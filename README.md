# Vet Cancer Treatment Finder

**Free veterinary oncology search tool for dog and cat cancer clinical trials and evidence-screened treatment options.**

Vet Cancer Treatment Finder helps pet owners search for treatment-focused **canine cancer clinical trials**, **feline cancer clinical trials**, veterinary oncology studies, and selected non-routine cancer treatment options with current access. The database covers the USA, Canada, the UK, Europe, Brazil, and other locations represented by verified studies.

**Live app:** https://vet-cancer-trial-finder.streamlit.app/

## What it does

The Clinical Trial Finder uses species, cancer type, diagnosis certainty, disease status, metastases, prior treatment, and other known eligibility factors to screen for potentially relevant treatment trials. It is designed to avoid presenting clearly ineligible studies as false hope.

The Additional Oncology Options section covers selected treatments outside the clinical-trial catalog when they meet a higher evidence-and-access threshold.

The project focuses on **actual anticancer treatment opportunities**. Observational studies, diagnostic-only studies, biomarker/sample-collection projects, biobanks, research-only pharmacokinetic studies, and supportive-care studies without an anticancer treatment objective are excluded from treatment matching.

## Coverage

The catalog includes veterinary oncology studies and treatment options for dogs and cats across many cancer types, including lymphoma, mast cell tumors, melanoma, osteosarcoma, soft-tissue sarcoma, hemangiosarcoma, histiocytic sarcoma, squamous cell carcinoma, brain tumors, and other malignancies.

Trial status, eligibility rules, and treatment access can change. The database is reviewed regularly and source links are provided so owners can confirm current details with the study team or veterinary oncologist.

## Important

This is a screening and research tool, not medical advice and not a determination of clinical-trial eligibility. A match means a study may be worth contacting; final eligibility belongs to the study investigators. Standard veterinary oncology care may be appropriate even when no clinical trial or additional option matches.
