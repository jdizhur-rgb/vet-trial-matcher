Advanced treatments

Selected cancer treatments with a current access pathway outside ordinary clinical trials.

Catalog notice: Inclusion is not an endorsement or a recommendation. Discuss a treatment’s relevance, evidence, risks and alternatives with your veterinary oncology team.

9 options shown

Oncept

A DNA vaccine for dogs with oral melanoma after the tumor and affected lymph nodes have been controlled with surgery or radiation. It uses instructions for human tyrosinase, a pigment-related protein, to help the immune system recognize melanoma cells.

Oral melanoma
Evidence

USDA-licensed for stage II or III oral melanoma after local control. Published survival evidence is mixed; a comparative retrospective study found no survival benefit.

Access and limitations

USA — administered through a veterinary oncology team. It is used after local treatment and is not a vaccine to prevent cancer in healthy dogs.

Laverdia (verdinexor)

An oral treatment for dogs with lymphoma. It blocks XPO1, a protein that moves other proteins out of the cell nucleus, helping keep growth-control proteins where they can act against cancer cells.

LymphomaB-cell lymphomaT-cell lymphoma
Evidence

Fully FDA-approved in January 2026 for canine lymphoma. Approval does not establish that it provides the same outcome as a CHOP chemotherapy protocol.

Access and limitations

USA — prescription tablets given at home, with veterinary monitoring. Appetite loss, weight loss and digestive side effects can occur; handling and dosing instructions matter.

Tanovea (rabacfosadine)

An intravenous chemotherapy drug for dogs with lymphoma. It interferes with DNA copying in cancer cells and is given at the veterinary clinic to help shrink or control the disease.

LymphomaB-cell lymphomaT-cell lymphoma
Evidence

Fully FDA-approved for canine lymphoma since 2021, with controlled clinical evidence. It is an established option, rather than a newly launched treatment.

Access and limitations

USA — administered by a veterinary team, with blood-count and other safety checks. Serious, sometimes fatal lung fibrosis is a known risk. It must not be used in West Highland White Terriers or dogs with chronic lung disease that could lead to fibrosis.

APAVAC / Vaxkit

A vaccine made from the dog’s own tumor sample, used alongside chemotherapy for B-cell lymphoma. It exposes the immune system to tumor proteins to help it recognize cancer cells.

B-cell lymphoma
Evidence

Randomized clinical evidence + comparative clinical cohort evidence

Comparative clinical evidence includes randomized and cohort data.

Access and limitations

USA / Canada / Europe — veterinarian-directed autologous vaccine platform.

Not a contemporary standard-of-care replacement.

Aurelius adoptive T-cell therapy

An experimental immune-cell treatment for B-cell lymphoma. T cells from the dog’s blood are grown and activated in a laboratory, then returned by intravenous infusion to help attack cancer cells.

B-cell lymphoma
Evidence

Published canine clinical evidence + current experimental access

Published canine clinical evidence supports adoptive T-cell transfer after chemotherapy in B-cell lymphoma.

Access and limitations

USA — veterinarian-directed experimental treatment from Aurelius Biotherapeutics in Bellingham, Washington.

Experimental therapy; USDA licensure is pending.

ELIAS Cancer Immunotherapy (ECI)

An immune treatment for dogs with osteosarcoma after amputation. A vaccine made from the removed tumor primes the immune response; the dog’s T cells are then collected, activated and returned to help target cancer cells elsewhere in the body.

Osteosarcoma
Evidence

USDA-approved autologous prescription product + canine clinical evidence

USDA licensure is supported by canine safety and efficacy studies.

Access and limitations

USA — veterinarian-directed ELIAS Cancer Immunotherapy (ECI).

Requires amputation and usable autologous tumor material.

Gilvetmab

An antibody treatment for dogs with mast cell tumors or malignant melanoma. It blocks PD-1, a signal that can hold back immune cells, helping them attack the cancer.

Evidence

Regulated veterinary therapy + prospective clinical data

Prospective multicenter clinical efficacy data support activity in mast cell tumors and malignant melanoma.

Access and limitations

USA — available through specialists practicing veterinary oncology under USDA conditional licensure.

The published efficacy study was single-arm.

Radioactive iodine (I-131)

Radioactive iodine is taken up by some thyroid cancers and delivers radiation inside the tumor. It may help when surgery cannot remove the cancer completely or it has spread, but only if a scan shows enough iodine uptake.

Thyroid carcinomaThyroid tumor / carcinoma
Evidence

Published canine clinical evidence + current specialty-center access

Published canine case series support meaningful tumor control and prolonged survival in selected dogs with non-resectable or invasive thyroid carcinoma.

Access and limitations

USA — available at a limited number of nuclear-medicine centers, including University of Missouri Veterinary Health Center and Advanced Veterinary Medical Imaging (AVMI), Tustin, California.

Not every canine thyroid carcinoma takes up enough iodine for treatment. Recent iodinated CT contrast can interfere with uptake; candidacy and timing must be determined by the treating center.

Toceranib (Palladia)

An oral targeted drug that blocks signals involved in tumor growth and blood-vessel formation. Veterinary oncologists sometimes use it off-label to help control thyroid carcinoma.

Thyroid carcinomaThyroid tumor / carcinoma
Evidence

Published multicenter retrospective canine clinical evidence

A multicenter retrospective study of 42 dogs with thyroid carcinoma reported clinical benefit in both treatment-naive and previously treated dogs.

Access and limitations

USA — prescription treatment managed by a veterinary oncologist; no single research center is required.

Off-label for canine thyroid carcinoma; retrospective evidence rather than a randomized trial. Response is not guaranteed and adverse effects require veterinary monitoring.
