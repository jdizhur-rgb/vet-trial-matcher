Genomic tests

Tests of a tumor’s DNA that may help your veterinarian consider treatment options. Testing does not treat cancer or guarantee that a suggested drug will help.

Catalog notice: Inclusion is not an endorsement or a recommendation. Discuss whether testing is useful, what the results can and cannot show, and its cost with your veterinary oncology team.

Vidium SearchLight DNA

Tumor genomic profiling of 120 cancer-related genes to identify diagnostic, prognostic and therapeutic biomarkers and potential mutation-matched drugs.

Cancer — any type
Evidence

Commercial targeted tumor sequencing + treatment guidance; strength of evidence varies by biomarker and drug

SearchLight DNA reports identify mutation-associated treatment options and can also flag therapies predicted to be ineffective or potentially harmful. Vidium provides targeted-drug monographs and complimentary veterinarian consultations for SearchLight DNA cases.

Access and limitations

Veterinarian-ordered genomic test. Vidium currently accepts international orders; treatment is selected and prescribed separately by the veterinary team.

This is a genomic diagnostic and treatment-guidance service, not a treatment provider. Some treatment associations rely partly on human oncology or limited canine evidence, and a genomic match does not establish clinical benefit for an individual dog.

VetOmics Canine CGP

Whole-exome tumor genomic profiling across 20,257 coding genes, with genomics-guided treatment recommendations.

Cancer — any type
Evidence

Commercial comprehensive genomic profiling + treatment guidance; actionability varies by finding

VetOmics provides clinical interpretation and potential therapeutic recommendations from tumor genomic findings. Actionability and canine-specific evidence vary by finding.

Access and limitations

USA — ordered through a veterinarian or veterinary hospital. VetOmics lists the Canine CGP price as $900 to hospitals/veterinarians; the hospital may add its own markup. Treatment is separate.

This is a genomic testing and treatment-guidance service, not a treatment provider. A genomic match does not establish that a recommended therapy will work in an individual dog; canine-specific evidence is limited for some matches.

Cancer — any type
Evidence

Commercial precision-oncology service + cancer-specific real-world clinical evidence

FidoCure provides tumor genomic profiling and treatment guidance across canine cancers. Cancer-specific clinical evidence varies; published real-world comparative evidence is available for splenic hemangiosarcoma.

USA — ordered and managed through the treating veterinarian/oncologist; treatment and medications are separate from testing.

This is genomic testing and treatment guidance, not a guarantee that a matched therapy will be effective. The strongest published outcome evidence is cancer-specific and should not be generalized to every tumor type.
