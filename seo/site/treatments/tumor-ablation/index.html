Tumor ablation clinical trials for dogs and cats

Tumor ablation destroys tissue locally using focused ultrasound, mechanical energy, freezing, heat or another physical method. Current listings include HIFU, histotripsy, cryotherapy, microwave and radiofrequency approaches.

15current studies and treatment programs in this category

These are local treatments. Their usefulness depends on the tumor’s size, location and accessibility, and they do not automatically address cancer elsewhere in the body.

Histotripsy for canine thyroid tumors

Virginia-Maryland College of Veterinary Medicine / Virginia Tech · USA

Recruiting — listed on Virginia Tech current studies page

What is being offered: Dogs must have a thyroid tumor whose owners have elected surgical removal, and must be stable enough for general anesthesia. Non-resectable tumors and other life-threatening disease are excluded. Eligible dogs receive one histotripsy treatment followed by surgical resection the next day.

Who may qualify: Active tumor required; Thyroid tumor; Planned surgery

May not qualify if: Unresectable tumor; Anesthesia contraindicated; Other life threatening disease

Costs / coverage: 🟡 Partially funded — the study covers the pre-planning MRI and one histotripsy treatment and provides a $3,000 credit toward thyroid-tumor resection at Virginia Tech; standard surgical workup and remaining care are not stated as covered.

Contact: Robyn Fox, LVT, Clinical Trials Coordinator — robynfox@vt.edu

Cryotherapy or intratumoral immunotherapy before amputation for canine osteosarcoma

Johns Hopkins Center for Image-Guided Animal Therapy (CIGAT) · USA

Open and recruiting — listed on the current Johns Hopkins CIGAT clinical-trials page

What is being offered: Treatment trial for histopathologically confirmed canine osteosarcoma. Eligible dogs receive PET-CT and either cryotherapy or two doses of tumor-injected immunotherapy before required amputation at about two weeks, followed by chemotherapy. Johns Hopkins lists no prior osteosarcoma treatment or chemotherapy for other cancers and requires the dog to be systemically healthy apart from osteosarcoma.

Who may qualify: Confirmed cancer diagnosis required; Active tumor required; Planned amputation and chemo

May not qualify if: Prior chemo; Prior osteosarcoma treatment

Costs / coverage: 🟡 Partially funded. Study covers PET-CT/CT imaging, cryotherapy or intratumoral immunotherapy, study-related medications and follow-up imaging/blood tests; required amputation and chemotherapy are owner-paid.

Contact: Johns Hopkins CIGAT — 410-502-7325; contact through the official clinical-trials page
