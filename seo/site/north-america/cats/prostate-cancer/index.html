About Prostate Cancer

Prostatic carcinoma is an aggressive cancer in dogs that can invade the urinary tract and spread to lymph nodes, lungs or bone. Because of the prostate’s location and the biology of the disease, complete surgical removal is often difficult.

How it is usually treated

Treatment is commonly multimodal and may include NSAIDs, chemotherapy and radiation. Procedures to maintain urine flow can be important when obstruction develops. Surgery is reserved for selected cases, and clinical trials may provide additional systemic or local options.

What can affect treatment choices

Urinary function, local extent, lymph-node, lung or bone metastasis, pain, previous treatment and whether the disease can be safely targeted with local therapy all influence treatment decisions.
