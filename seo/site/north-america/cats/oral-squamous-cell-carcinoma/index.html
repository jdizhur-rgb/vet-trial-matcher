About Oral Squamous Cell Carcinoma

Oral squamous cell carcinoma is a locally invasive cancer arising from the lining of the mouth. It can involve the jaw, tongue or surrounding tissues. In cats it is often especially difficult to control locally because many tumors are advanced by the time they are diagnosed.

How it is usually treated

Surgery offers the best local control when complete removal is anatomically possible. Radiation, including definitive or palliative treatment, may be considered for tumors that cannot be fully removed. Systemic treatments have more limited and variable roles, which is one reason new local, targeted and immune approaches are actively studied.

What can affect treatment choices

Treatment choices depend on species, exact oral location, tumor size, bone involvement, lymph-node or distant spread, surgical feasibility, pain and eating function, and previous treatment.
