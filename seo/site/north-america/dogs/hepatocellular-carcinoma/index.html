About Hepatocellular Carcinoma

Hepatocellular carcinoma is a primary cancer of liver cells. In dogs, a single large liver mass can behave very differently from multifocal or diffuse disease. Imaging is important for determining how much of the liver is involved and whether the tumor appears surgically removable.

How it is usually treated

Surgery is generally preferred for a resectable solitary liver mass and can provide strong local control. Multifocal, diffuse, unresectable or metastatic disease requires a different plan and may lead to discussion of systemic, local interventional or investigational treatments.

What can affect treatment choices

The number and location of liver lesions, involvement of major vessels, surgical resectability, liver function, pathology and evidence of metastasis are central to treatment planning.
