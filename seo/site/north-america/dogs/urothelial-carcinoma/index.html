Urothelial Carcinoma: Clinical Trials and Cancer Treatment Studies for Dogs in USA & Canada

About Urothelial Carcinoma

Urothelial carcinoma, historically called transitional cell carcinoma, most often affects the urinary bladder or urethra and can obstruct urine flow or spread to lymph nodes and distant organs.

Standard treatment

Medical treatment is common because many bladder tumors are not amenable to complete surgery. NSAID therapy combined with chemotherapy is frequently used in dogs; radiation, surgery or interventional procedures may be appropriate in selected cases. Treatment depends on tumor location, obstruction and metastatic stage.

Intravesical IL-12 immunotherapy for canine invasive bladder cancer

NC State College of Veterinary Medicine · USA

Actively recruiting — enrollment finish September 30, 2026

Treatment: Experimental intravesical IL-12 immunotherapy. Metastatic UCC is permitted. Untreated and previously treated dogs may qualify; at least one month is required since last radiation or chemotherapy. No placebo group; all enrolled dogs receive immunotherapy.

Key eligibility: Confirmed; Active tumor required; Min weight kg: 8; Muscle invasive bladder mass

Important exclusions: Current prednisone; Prior immunotherapy

Costs: Pre-staging, IL-12 immunotherapy, hospitalization, anesthesia and study monitoring are provided at no cost.
