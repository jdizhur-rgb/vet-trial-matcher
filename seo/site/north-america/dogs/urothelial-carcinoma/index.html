About Urothelial Carcinoma

Urothelial carcinoma, historically called transitional cell carcinoma, most often affects the urinary bladder or urethra. Its location can make complete surgical removal difficult, and progression can interfere with normal urine flow. It may also spread to lymph nodes or distant organs.

How it is usually treated

Medical treatment is common and often includes an NSAID together with chemotherapy in dogs. Radiation, surgery or interventional procedures can be useful in selected cases, particularly when local disease or urinary obstruction needs to be addressed. Clinical trials may offer additional drug or targeted-treatment options.

What can affect treatment choices

Treatment choices depend on the exact bladder or urethral location, ability to urinate normally, local invasion, lymph-node or distant metastasis, kidney function, previous drugs and whether measurable disease is present.
