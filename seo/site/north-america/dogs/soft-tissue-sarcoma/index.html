About Soft Tissue Sarcoma

Soft tissue sarcoma is a group of cancers that arise from connective and supporting tissues such as muscle, fibrous tissue, nerves and blood-vessel-associated tissues. These tumors often grow into nearby tissue rather than staying neatly separated from it. Their risk of spreading elsewhere varies considerably with subtype and grade.

How it is usually treated

For a removable tumor, wide surgical excision is usually the main local treatment. If margins are incomplete or narrow and another surgery is not practical, radiation can be considered to improve local control. Chemotherapy is more often discussed for high-grade, metastatic or otherwise high-risk tumors. Some centers also offer investigational local treatments, immunotherapy or other trial options.

What can affect treatment choices

The most useful details for treatment planning are the exact subtype when known, grade, tumor size and location, whether clean surgical margins are achievable, margin status after surgery, measurable residual disease and evidence of metastasis.
