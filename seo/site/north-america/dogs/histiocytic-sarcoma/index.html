Histiocytic Sarcoma: Clinical Trials and Cancer Treatment Studies for Dogs in USA & Canada

About Histiocytic Sarcoma

Histiocytic sarcoma is an aggressive cancer of histiocytic immune cells. In dogs it may arise as a localized mass or as disseminated disease involving multiple organs, and metastatic risk is substantial. Staging commonly includes chest imaging and abdominal imaging, with additional tests guided by the primary site.

Standard treatment

When disease is localized and removable, surgery is generally the main local treatment; radiation may be considered when complete surgical control is not possible. Because microscopic or distant spread is common, systemic chemotherapy—most often lomustine (CCNU) in canine practice—is frequently considered. Treatment is individualized by site, stage, margins and the patient’s overall health.

Phase II trametinib for dogs with histiocytic sarcoma

University of Florida · USA

Currently enrolling

Treatment: Multicenter Phase II trametinib study. Published recruitment criteria require a measurable tumor, no concurrent anticancer therapy, at least a 2-week washout after chemotherapy, and at least a 4-week washout after radiation therapy.

Key eligibility: Confirmed; Active tumor required; Measurable; chemotherapy washout: 14 days; radiation washout: 28 days

Important exclusions: Current chemo; Current radiation

Costs: See official study page.

Contact: UF Clinical Studies — complete the Study Interest Form on the official trial page; UF Oncology Staff: (352) 392-2235

Quasiorganelle therapy for canine histiocytic sarcoma

Michigan State University Veterinary Medical Center · USA

Actively recruiting — current MSU clinical-trials page and August 24, 2026 recruitment announcement

Treatment: First cohort receives weakened non-disease-causing Listeria-derived quasiorganelle therapy alone; second cohort receives quasiorganelle therapy including gene therapy. Up to 5 visible lesions are allowed with summed longest dimensions under 15 cm. Weekly treatment x4, then 2 weeks off, repeated for a total 16-week treatment period. While enrolled, dogs should not live with someone pregnant or planning pregnancy.

Key eligibility: Confirmed; Active tumor required; Measurable; Visible tumor; Min weight kg: 20; Max vcog performance status: 2

Important exclusions: Active wounds; Immunodeficiency; Immune mediated disease; Recent systemic antibiotics 14d; Recent immunosuppressants 14d; Recent prednisone 7d; Recent chemotherapy 14d; Recent radiation 30d

Costs: Funded trial. Trial-associated tests, quasiorganelle treatment, hospitalization, blood/fecal sampling, imaging and treatment of trial-related complications are covered after enrollment; initial evaluation is charged.

Contact: MSU Medical Oncology Team — (517) 353-5420; cvm.oncoinfo@msu.edu
