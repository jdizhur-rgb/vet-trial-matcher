Hemangiosarcoma: Clinical Trials and Cancer Treatment Studies for Dogs in USA & Canada

About Hemangiosarcoma

Hemangiosarcoma is an aggressive malignant tumor of blood-vessel-forming cells. In dogs it commonly affects the spleen, heart or liver and can rupture and bleed; microscopic metastasis is often present even when imaging does not show it.

Standard treatment

For a resectable primary tumor, surgery is used for local control and to obtain a diagnosis. Because metastatic risk is high, adjuvant systemic chemotherapy—commonly doxorubicin-based in dogs—is often recommended. Treatment differs for cutaneous forms and depends on site, stage and whether rupture or metastasis has occurred.

Ethos-PUSH precision-medicine umbrella study for splenic hemangiosarcoma

Ethos Discovery / multicenter specialty hospitals · USA

Open / currently enrolling

Treatment: Phase 1 splenectomy/hemoabdomen study; dogs confirmed with non-metastatic splenic HSA can continue to fully funded active-treatment Phase 2, with treatment assigned among doxorubicin, doxorubicin+rapamycin, doxorubicin+sorafenib, or oral paclitaxel regimens.

Key eligibility: Min weight lb: 11.0; No metastasis; Max weight lb: 99.2; Active tumor required

Costs: $1,000 toward splenectomy; up to $3,000 for study-related adverse-event treatment; confirmed non-metastatic HSA treatment phase fully funded (> $10,000 stated value).

Contact: Ethos Discovery enrollment form / participating hospital; CASE: 303-545-2273
