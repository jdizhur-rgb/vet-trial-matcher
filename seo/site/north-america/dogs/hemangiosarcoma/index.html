About Hemangiosarcoma

Hemangiosarcoma is a malignant cancer associated with blood-vessel-forming cells. In dogs it commonly arises in the spleen, heart or liver, although it can occur in other sites. Internal tumors can rupture and bleed, and microscopic spread may already be present even when imaging does not show visible metastases. Skin forms can behave differently from internal disease.

How it is usually treated

When an internal primary tumor can be safely removed, surgery is used for local control and definitive diagnosis. Because systemic spread is a major concern, chemotherapy, commonly doxorubicin-based in dogs, is often discussed after surgery. Treatment for cutaneous disease depends on how deeply the tumor extends. Clinical trials may investigate new systemic, immune or targeted approaches.

What can affect treatment choices

Treatment planning depends heavily on the primary site, whether rupture or bleeding has occurred, pathology, stage, visible metastasis, whether the primary tumor can be removed and the patient’s cardiovascular and overall condition.
