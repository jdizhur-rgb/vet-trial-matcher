About Osteosarcoma

Osteosarcoma is the most common primary bone cancer in dogs. It damages normal bone, causing pain and increasing the risk of fracture. Even when the primary tumor appears localized, microscopic metastatic disease is a major concern, most commonly involving the lungs.

How it is usually treated

For osteosarcoma of a limb, amputation or limb-sparing surgery can provide local control and pain relief. Systemic chemotherapy, commonly a platinum-based drug such as carboplatin, is usually discussed because of metastatic risk. When surgery is not appropriate, stereotactic or palliative radiation and other pain-control strategies can be used. Several immunotherapy and other investigational approaches are also being studied.

What can affect treatment choices

Key factors include tumor location, whether the bone can be surgically treated, visible lung or other metastases, pathologic fracture, pain and mobility, previous therapy and the dog’s ability to tolerate local and systemic treatment.
