About Penn Vet Cancer Research

Penn Vet combines comprehensive cancer care at Ryan Hospital with the Penn Vet Cancer Center and the Veterinary Clinical Investigations Center. Its program links laboratory cancer biology, clinical oncology and comparative medicine, with clinical trials used to evaluate novel therapies in companion animals.

Penn Vet research spans medical oncology, radiation, surgery and immuno-oncology. The Mason Immunotherapy Research Laboratory, led by Nicola Mason, BVetMed, PhD, develops cellular immunotherapies including CAR-based approaches for canine cancers, while Penn Vet's oncology service participates in comparative oncology research.

CAR-iNKT cell therapy for canine metastatic osteosarcoma

University of Pennsylvania School of Veterinary Medicine — Comparative Immunotherapy Program · USA

Recruiting — listed as a current trial by Penn Vet Comparative Immunotherapy Program

What is being offered: Phase I cell-therapy study using donor-derived invariant natural killer T (iNKT) cells engineered with a chimeric antigen receptor (CAR). The study evaluates safety, maximum tolerated dose, persistence, and antitumor activity against canine osteosarcoma metastatic to the lungs. Full protocol eligibility requires Penn prescreening.

Who may qualify: Confirmed; Active tumor required; Lung metastases

Costs / coverage: Confirm study-covered and owner-paid costs with the Penn Vet study team.

Contact: Nicola Mason, BVetMed, PhD, DACVIM, FRCVS — nmason@vet.upenn.edu; (215) 898-3996

Proton FLASH radiation therapy for canine extremity soft tissue sarcoma

University of Pennsylvania School of Veterinary Medicine · USA

Current Penn Vet clinical trial

What is being offered: Three proton FLASH-RT treatments over 5–9 days for extremity STS where surgery is not feasible or has been declined. Prior surgery is acceptable.

Who may qualify: Confirmed; Active tumor required; Extremity sts; Max tumor cm: 10; No metastasis

May not qualify if: Pulmonary metastasis; Regional lymph node metastasis

Costs / coverage: Sponsor covers all study-related procedures including three FLASH-RT treatments, examinations, chest radiographs, laboratory tests, sedation/anesthesia and CT; screening visit is owner-paid.

B7H3-targeted CAR-iNKT cell therapy for newly diagnosed canine osteosarcoma

Penn Vet Comparative Immunotherapy Program · USA

Recruiting; investigator prescreen required

What is being offered: Investigational B7H3-targeted CAR-iNKT cell therapy for dogs with newly diagnosed osteosarcoma. This is a distinct protocol from Penn's CAR-iNKT study for dogs with metastatic osteosarcoma. Treatment is followed by amputation and standard-of-care chemotherapy; full eligibility requires Penn Vet prescreening.

Who may qualify: Confirmed; Active tumor required; Newly diagnosed

Costs / coverage: 🟢 **Fully funded** — Including initial screening; no cost to owners. Screening, confirmatory biopsy/B7H3 testing, CAR-iNKT therapy, amputation/post-op care, standard chemotherapy and study monitoring are covered.

Contact: Penn Vet Comparative Immunotherapy Program — contact through the current study page
