About the NC State Animal Cancer Center

The Animal Cancer Center at NC State brings medical, surgical and radiation oncology together with laboratory and clinical research. Its comparative oncology program develops and tests new cancer diagnostics and therapies for veterinary patients while also studying discoveries that may translate across species.

NC State cancer research spans genomics, immunotherapy, radiation sciences, precision medicine, pathology and bioelectric therapies. Investigators include Paul Hess, Michael Nolan, Mike Sano and Matthew Breen among clinician-scientists working across multiple cancer laboratories.

Intravesical IL-12 immunotherapy for canine invasive bladder cancer

NC State College of Veterinary Medicine · USA

Actively recruiting — enrollment finish September 30, 2026

What is being offered: Experimental intravesical IL-12 immunotherapy. Metastatic UCC is permitted. Untreated and previously treated dogs may qualify; at least one month is required since last radiation or chemotherapy. No placebo group; all enrolled dogs receive immunotherapy.

Who may qualify: Confirmed; Active tumor required; Min weight kg: 8; Muscle invasive bladder mass

May not qualify if: Current prednisone; Prior immunotherapy

Costs / coverage: `Fully funded` Pre-staging, IL-12 immunotherapy, hospitalization, anesthesia and study monitoring are provided at no cost.

Radiation therapy plus XCSgel immunotherapy for canine soft tissue sarcoma

NC State College of Veterinary Medicine · USA

Actively recruiting — enrollment finish August 31, 2027

What is being offered: Blinded study of standard radiation therapy plus XCSgel immunotherapy; all enrolled dogs receive surgery and radiation, while XCSgel versus placebo is blinded. Pathologic confirmation of STS required and no prior radiation or chemotherapy.

Who may qualify: Confirmed; Active tumor required; Measurable; Min tumor cm: 4; Max tumor cm: 10

May not qualify if: Prior chemo; Prior local radiation

Costs / coverage: `Fully funded` NC State states enrolled dogs receive free testing, treatment and follow-up, including bloodwork, CT, surgery and radiation therapy.

PIVOT-C intratumoral FVRCP vaccine therapy for feline oral tumors

NC State College of Veterinary Medicine · USA

Actively recruiting — enrollment finish December 31, 2027

What is being offered: 28-day pilot treatment study with weekly intratumoral FVRCP vaccine injections on days 0, 7, 14, 21 and 28. Cats cannot receive surgery or radiation while enrolled; those treatments may be considered after study completion or early exit.

Who may qualify: Active tumor required; Oral mass

May not qualify if: Concurrent surgery; Concurrent local radiation

Costs / coverage: Confirm study-covered and owner-paid costs with the investigator.

Comparative radiotherapy approaches before amputation for canine osteosarcoma

NC State College of Veterinary Medicine · USA

Actively recruiting — enrollment finish December 31, 2026

What is being offered: Large or giant breed dogs with a distal-radius bone tumor may qualify. Each enrolled dog receives a single dose of radiotherapy followed within three weeks by standard limb amputation. No prior osteosarcoma-directed therapy; no metastasis.

Who may qualify: Confirmed; Active tumor required; Distal radius location; No metastasis

May not qualify if: Prior chemo; Prior osteosarcoma treatment; Pathologic fracture

Costs / coverage: `Fully funded` NC State states enrolled dogs receive free bloodwork, biopsy, CT, radiation therapy and limb-amputation surgery; postoperative chemotherapy is not stated as covered.

Contact: NC State Clinical Studies Core — see official trial page
