About the UC Davis Veterinary Center for Clinical Trials

The UC Davis Veterinary Center for Clinical Trials coordinates clinical research at the Veterinary Medical Teaching Hospital and connects veterinary patients with active studies across oncology and other specialties.

Cancer studies are coordinated with the school's oncology programs and Center for Companion Animal Health. The VCCT is directed by veterinary oncologist Robert Rebhun, DVM, PhD, whose research focuses on cancer metastasis and comparative oncology.

CARE trial: autophagy inhibition plus radiation for canine glioma

UC Davis Weill School of Veterinary Medicine / Translational Brain Tumor Center · USA

Actively recruiting — UC Davis recruitment announcement published September 3, 2026

What is being offered: Combined Autophagy inhibition and Radiation to improve Efficacy in canine glioma (CARE). Dogs receive an experimental IV autophagy-inhibiting drug twice weekly for four weeks concurrently with standard radiation therapy, with brain-tumor biopsy before and after treatment. Current UC Davis materials state dogs with MRI-suspected glioma may be screened; final eligibility is determined by the neuro-oncology team.

Who may qualify: Active tumor required; Suspected or confirmed glioma; Radiation therapy; Brain tumor biopsy

Costs / coverage: Study covers all study procedures and provides $4,000 toward radiation therapy; owners remain responsible for radiation costs beyond the study contribution.

Contact: UC Davis Veterinary Center for Clinical Trials — (530) 754-1954; contact Dr. Christine Toedebusch / study team via the official UC Davis page
