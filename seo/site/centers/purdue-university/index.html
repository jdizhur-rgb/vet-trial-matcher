About Purdue Comparative Oncology

Purdue's Comparative Oncology Program conducts clinical trials in pet dogs with naturally occurring cancer through the Purdue University Veterinary Hospital. Trials are designed to test new cancer treatments and diagnostics while maintaining quality of life and may provide partial or full study support for eligible patients.

Purdue comparative oncology research includes lymphoma, bladder cancer and other naturally occurring canine cancers. Michael Childress, DVM, is Professor of Comparative Oncology, and the program coordinates cancer-trial enrollment through dedicated clinical research staff.

AKS-701d anti-PD-L1 monoclonal antibody for canine invasive urothelial carcinoma

Purdue University Veterinary Hospital / Werling Comparative Oncology Research Center · USA

Enrollment ongoing in 2026

What is being offered: NCI-funded Purdue-directed exploratory treatment trial of the caninized anti-PD-L1 monoclonal antibody AKS-701d in client-owned dogs with histopathologically confirmed invasive urothelial carcinoma. Public 2026 development disclosures state enrollment is ongoing; Purdue's current cancer-research page identifies the anti-cPD-L1 bladder-cancer trial as active research.

Who may qualify: Confirmed; Active tumor required; Measurable; Cystoscopy accessible mass; Serum creatinine lt 2; Life expectancy weeks min: 6

May not qualify if: High urethral obstruction risk

Costs / coverage: Confirm current study-covered and owner-paid costs with Purdue.

Contact: Purdue Veterinary Hospital / Werling Comparative Oncology Research Center — contact through Purdue current oncology clinical trials
