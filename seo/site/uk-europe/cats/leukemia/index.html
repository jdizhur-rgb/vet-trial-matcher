About Leukemia

Leukemia is a cancer of blood-forming cells. Acute and chronic leukemias are biologically different diseases, and identifying the cell type is essential because treatment intensity and expected response can differ greatly.

How it is usually treated

Acute leukemias generally require systemic multi-drug chemotherapy and substantial supportive care. Some chronic leukemias can be managed for longer periods with oral chemotherapy or targeted drugs. The exact protocol depends on whether the leukemia is lymphoid or myeloid, acute or chronic, and on the patient’s blood counts and clinical condition.

What can affect treatment choices

Acute versus chronic disease, lymphoid versus myeloid origin, blood and bone-marrow findings, cytopenias, organ involvement, previous therapy and overall clinical stability are key treatment factors.
