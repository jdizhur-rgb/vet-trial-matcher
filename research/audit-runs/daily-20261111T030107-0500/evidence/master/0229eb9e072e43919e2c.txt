An estimated one in four dogs will develop cancer during their lifetime, with risk increasing significantly in dogs over the age of six. Despite this high prevalence, treatment options often remain limited, burdensome, or ineffective, especially for aggressive tumor types. Benno Therapeutics is dedicated to changing this. We are developing a new class of immunotherapies based on the iBOOST platform, originally created at Amsterdam UMC, to provide safer and more effective cancer treatments for dogs. A Novel Approach: Targeting Tumor Blood Vessels, Not the Tumor Traditional cancer treatments focus on destroying tumor cells directly. However, cancer cells mutate rapidly, often developing resistance to therapy. Benno Therapeutics takes a fundamentally different approach: We target the genetically stable blood vessels that tumors need to survive and grow. Our lead candidate, BTX001 , is a therapeutic vaccine designed to train the immune system to attack tumor-associated blood vessels. It does this by inducing antibodies against externalized vimentin (eVim ) —a self-protein that becomes exposed on the surface of activated endothelial cells in tumor vasculature, but not on healthy tissues. To break immune tolerance, eVim is fused to a foreign carrier. This allows the vaccinated dog to generate strong antibody responses that: Destroy tumor blood vessels Suppress tumor growth Limit metastatic spread Importantly, extensive safety data show that this targeted approach is very safe, making BTX001 a promising therapeutic option for many cancer types. Lead Indication: Canine Bladder Cancer Canine bladder cancer is a severe and life-threatening disease with no approved veterinary treatments. Current care typically relies on COX-2 inhibitors, radiation therapy, and chemotherapy, all of which have limited efficacy and can cause significant side effects. Chemotherapy also poses risks to owners due to the handling of toxic agents. In an exploratory study in pet dogs, BTX001 demonstrated: An excellent safety profile A significantly improved survival rate compared with traditional treatments Because BTX001 works by targeting tumor vasculature, a universal feature of solid tumors, it has the potential to benefit dogs with multiple cancer types , not just bladder cancer. Promising Results in Hemangiosarcoma Visceral hemangiosarcoma is one of the most aggressive canine cancers. Because the disease progresses rapidly, vaccination alone does not allow enough time to build protective antibody levels. Instead, BTX001 was evaluated in combination with doxorubicin (DOX). In a recently published prospective study, dogs receiving BTX001 + DOX achieved: Increased median survival from 136 to 235 days Significantly improved 1-year restricted mean survival A 1-year survival rate of 44%, compared with 14% in DOX-only controls No added toxicity from the vaccine These results highlight the flexibility of the platform BTX001 can be used alone for slower-progressing tumors, or alongside chemotherapy when needed. Expanding into Other Cancers: Osteosarcoma Osteosarcoma is an aggressive bone cancer where survival after amputation alone averages around 136 days, and without surgery only 1–3 months a study in patient dogs receiving amputation plus BTX001 is ongoing. A Broad Platform for Multiple Tumor Types Benno Therapeutics’ strategy is based on a simple but powerful principle: One mechanism, multiple cancers. Because tumor blood vessels share the same biological features across cancer types, BTX001 may offer benefit across a wide spectrum of canine tumors.
