Get involved OUR APPROACH Translated from human medicine. Many diseases in dogs and cats share underlying biology with human disease. Familiar starts with drug candidates whose targets and mechanisms are already understood in people, then does the translational work required to determine whether they can become veterinary medicines. Before a program advances, we confirm target biology, pharmacokinetics, dosing, and clinical feasibility in the animals we intend to treat. Where we can, we pursue medicines designed to change the course of the disease, not only manage its signs. FROM PIPELINE TO PRACTICE Designed to work in everyday veterinary practice. 01 Oral-dose small molecules Where the biology allows, we favor oral small molecules: medicines you can prescribe in a telehealth consult and have delivered directly to your client’s door. 02 Scalable from the start We assess manufacturability and scalability early, so promising programs can reach more patients. 03 Developed with a companion test Wherever possible, we pair a program with a companion test, so patient selection doesn't depend on a referral. 04 Shaped by practicing veterinarians We develop our programs with input from veterinarians in real-world clinical practice, including general practitioners. Study details and eligibility → Clinical trials Join our clinical-trial network. Familiar's trials are designed to run in client-owned animals with naturally occurring disease, generating evidence in the patients veterinarians see every day. We're building a network of general practices and specialty centers to support these studies as our programs advance. As studies open across our therapeutic areas, network practices will be among the first we contact about opportunities to participate. What participation looks like Familiar-funded We sponsor and fund the study. Client-owned patients Trials enroll animals with naturally occurring disease treated in clinical practice. Clinically grounded protocols Studies are designed with our Scientific Advisory Board and participating investigators. Shared evidence Participating clinicians see the data they help generate.
