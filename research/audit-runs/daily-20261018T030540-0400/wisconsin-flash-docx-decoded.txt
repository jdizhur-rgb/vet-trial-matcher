FLASH radiotherapy Clinical trial for canine osteosarcoma
Purpose:  In collaboration with the UW Medical Physics Department, we are investigating the advantages of a novel radiotherapy technique called FLASH. FLASH radiation is defined as ultra-high dose rate radiation which has been shown in small animal and tissue models to spare normal tissues to a greater extent than normal radiation.

Who qualifies: Any dogs with suspect osteosarcoma on a limb. 

What happens: 1 treatment of radiation will be performed. Your dog will be randomized to either the FLASH experimental group or the control group receiving conventional radiation. 4 once-weekly follow-up visits are then performed to assess for side effects.

Financial incentives: Cost of radiation covered by the study.  Amputation is considered standard of care for osteosarcoma. If you elect to pursue amputation, this procedure will be performed 1 month after the start of the study and the cost will be covered by the study.
