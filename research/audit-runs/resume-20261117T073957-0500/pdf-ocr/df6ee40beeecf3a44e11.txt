Tumor Types Enrolling

- Melanomas

Inclusion Criteria

- Must have a confirmed active melanoma
tumor diagnosis

+ Must have a tumor amendable to receive
injection (IT chemo) and/or ultrasound
imaging (HIFU)

Exclusion Criteria

- Severe chronic diseases (e.g. some
cardiovascular diseases, history of
anesthetic complications) that increase
the risk for anesthetic or treatment
complications

- Prior chemotherapy treatments

Procedures

+ Questionnaire before consultation, and
questionnaires during treatment (every 3
weeks) and at each post-treatment visit
(at least at 3, 6, and 12 weeks)

- Physical exams at consultation,
treatment, and post-treatment visits

+ Sample collection (e.g. blood, urine,
feces, etc.) and possible imaging (MRI,
CT, X-ray, ultrasound, etc.) at
consultation, treatment, and post-
treatment visits. A CT or ultrasound will
be taken at 6-week post-treatment visit.

- Patients will be evaluated at 2 months
post-treatment to determine if additional
therapies need to be pursued and if
extended monitoring is needed.

*Clinicians may decide that your pet is in
need of additional samples/diagnostics at
any time throughout the study based on their
current health condition. Post-treatment
follow-up visits for 3 months are mandatory
for on-study pets.

Study #4

Veterinary Clinical Trials in Canine and
Feline Patients Using Combination Novel
Therapeutics, Devices, and Data
Collection Techniques

Study objective: To assess the safety, tumor response, and delivery efficiency of carboplatin
chemotherapy administered intratumorally (IT) (into the tumor) versus intravenously (IV) (into the veins),
with or without high-intensity focused ultrasound (HIFU) enhancement, in dogs with naturally occurring
melanoma. Carboplatin administered alone has limited efficacy and can cause systemic toxicity in
canines with melanoma. This study aims to evaluate whether combining carboplatin with HIFU
hyperthermia enhances intratumoral drug delivery, overcomes chemoresistance, and promotes the
release of tumor antigens to enhance the antitumor immune response via IT and IV routes.

Treatments

Arm1
+ Carboplatin: given IV every 3 weeks for 3-4 cycles (9-12 weeks)
« Without HIFU (hyperthermia): given concurrently with
chemotherapy for 9-12 weeks
Arm 2
+ Carboplatin: given IV every 3 weeks for 3-4 cycles (9-12 weeks)
« With HIFU (hyperthermia): given concurrently with chemotherapy
for 9-12 weeks
Arm 3
+ Carboplatin: given IT every 3 weeks for 3-4 cycles (9-12 weeks)
+ Without HIFU (hyperthermia): given concurrently with
chemotherapy for 9-12 weeks
Arm 4
« Carboplatin: given IT every 3 weeks for 3-4 cycles (9-12 weeks)
+ With HIFU (hyperthermia): given concurrently with chemotherapy
for 9-12 weeks

Treatment Timeline

Referring vet or client contacts VROC and
sends patient’s medical records.

VROC clinicians review patient’s medical records and

decide if they may be eligible for a clinical trial.

Potentially eligible patients are scheduled for a consultation
and consented to the Master protocol for tissue and data
collection to confirm cancer and assess their health.

After consultation, clinicians review the patient’s case and
determine which treatment arm, if any, they qualify for.

The clinician will discuss treatment options with owner
and owner can consent to start determined treatment plan
for the patient’s cancer.

This study will be fully paid for by owners. However, possible subsidies may be presented.
