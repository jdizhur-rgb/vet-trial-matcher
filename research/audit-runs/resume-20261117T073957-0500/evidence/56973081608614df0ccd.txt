    "description": "<p>Canine transmissible venereal tumor (TVT) is an infectious cancer that can be spread between dogs, often through grooming behaviors or sexual contact. This cancer can be found worldwide, but is most common in Central and South America, Africa, and Asia. In the United States, this cancer is most commonly seen in animals rescued from other parts of the world, or in free-roaming pets in very remote areas.</p><p><br></p><p>Treatment for this cancer involves once-weekly injections of chemotherapy, which are given until the cancer is resolved. For patients who cannot undergo this chemotherapy treatment, finding alternate therapies can be invaluable. We know that this cancer responds well to older forms of radiation therapy; however, we aim to determine if a single treatment with modernized radiation can provide similar treatment outcomes with fewer visits compared to chemotherapy.</p>",
    "requirements": "<p>Dogs enrolled in this study will receive a single dose of radiation therapy, followed by recheck visits 7 days and 28 days after treatment. At each of these visits, patients will have tumor measurements and small samples of their masses collected with a needle for analysis. Patients complete the study on day 28 or if their tumor progresses before the end of the study. At that time, patients with persistent tumors are eligible to receive $1000 towards the cost of standard chemotherapy treatment.</p>",
    "who_can_participate": "<p>Confirmed diagnosis of TVT with at least one lesion &gt; 1 cm in size and amenable to repeated measurement and sampling</p><p>Healthy enough to undergo general anesthesia</p><p>No prior surgery, radiation, or systemic chemotherapy for treatment of primary tumor</p><p>No advanced cancer spread</p><p>7-day washout from immunosuppressive medications</p>",
    "benefits_and_risks": "<p>Possible benefits include shrinkage or resolution of the tumor. Risks of anesthesia include a decrease in heart rate, decrease in blood pressure, vomiting, risk of aspiration pneumonia, and sudden death. Few to no side effects are expected with this radiation treatment. Possible short-term side effects could include irritation to the mucous membranes and skin. Rare, long-term side effects could include loss of fur, change in fur color, and mild thickening or scarring of the tissues in area of radiation treatment.</p>",
