    Clinical trial combining radiation and chemotherapy in
                   dogs with high-grade glioma:
             Information on participating in the trial




Diagnosis “glioma” – what now?
Dogs diagnosed with a brain tumor can confront their owners with a variety of symptoms, ranging from
behavioral changes to epileptic seizures. It is often assumed that the tumor is a so-called glioma. This
tumor, which originates in the supporting cells of the brain, is also frequently diagnosed in humans.
Around 75% of affected dogs suffer from an aggressive form – high-grade glioma.

The standard treatment for gliomas in humans is multimodal and consists of surgical removal, radiation
therapy, and chemotherapy. In dogs, however, radiation therapy is the most common form of treatment.
Chemotherapy is still not widely used for brain tumors in dogs.

Based on recent results in humans with gliomas, combined therapy using radiation therapy and
chemotherapy with lomustine and temozolomide can significantly prolong survival without
compromising quality of life. Lomustine and temozolomide are already used to treat other types of
tumors in dogs, and the tolerability of both drugs in dogs has already been investigated. In this study,
we want to investigate whether we can also significantly improve survival time in dogs with high-grade
gliomas without compromising their quality of life.

The radiotherapy
Radiotherapy takes place in 10 short treatment sessions on weekdays. The irradiation under short
anesthesia takes only a few minutes. From drop-off to pick-up, it takes about one to one and a half
hours for the animal to be fully awake and back on its feet. Most animals are brought in daily, but they
are also welcome to stay in the hospital during this time. Normally, we do not expect any side effects
during and shortly after radiation therapy. The treatment itself is not painful, and patients live a normal
life alongside their daily sessions. There is a small residual risk of so-called late effects of radiation
therapy. We rarely see these, as we are able to minimize the risk through careful therapy planning and
thanks to very precise radiation equipment.

The chemotherapy
For the purposes of our study, all participating dogs will be randomly divided into two groups. Both
groups will receive radiation therapy, which is the current standard treatment. However, half of the dogs
will receive chemotherapy with temozolomide and lomustine. The other half will receive a placebo.
Throughout the study, neither the owners nor the treating veterinarians will know which group each dog
belongs to. At each visit, the dogs will undergo a clinical examination and a blood test before receiving
chemotherapy or the placebo. The medication will be administered in capsule form. This can be done
either at home or by us at the hospital.

Advantages and disadvantages of participating in the study, safety measures
Dogs that are suitable for participation in our study have undergone thorough clinical and neurological
examinations. A comprehensive blood test confirms that there are no other serious illnesses. An MRI
scan of the brain allows us to make a tentative diagnosis of high-grade glioma. For the purposes of the
study, it is necessary to confirm this suspected diagnosis with a minimally invasive tumor biopsy before
starting treatment. Before starting any chemotherapy, your pet will be thoroughly examined again and
blood tests will be performed. The drugs will only be administered if the tests are normal.
Residues of the chemotherapy drug may be excreted in urine and feces for several days and may also
be found in other bodily fluids such as vomit or blood. When cleaning up urine, feces, and vomit, owners
should therefore wear gloves (during the chemotherapy period) and ensure that these do not come into
contact with children or pregnant women.
By participating in the study, you will be supporting our research and future patients, and you will benefit
from a reduced price for radiation therapy. The brain biopsy and the costs of chemotherapy, including
the associated laboratory tests, are free of charge for study participants.
