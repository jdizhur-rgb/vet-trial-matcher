Purpose
To evaluate a new treatment called photodynamic therapy (PDT) for dogs who have been diagnosed with liver tumors.  The goal is to find out if PDT can be done safely, and see if it helps destroy the tumor cells in the liver.  The long-term objective is to develop a minimally invasive way of treating liver tumors.
Background
Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is the most common type of liver cancer diagnosed in dogs. While current treatments like radiation and targeted chemotherapy are available, they unfortunately rarely eliminate the tumors completely.  Surgical removal of the tumor and affected portion of the liver remains the standard of care option; however, not every patient is eligible for surgery.
To provide better options for pets, our research team is evaluating a groundbreaking new treatment called Photodynamic Therapy utilizing Light-Activatable Sustained-Exposure Ethanol Injection Technology—or LASEIT-PDT. This innovative technology has the potential to significantly advance how we treat canine liver cancer.
Eligibility
dogs recently diagnosed with a liver tumordogs whose families have elected surgical removal of the liver tumor

Study Design
Patients who have undergone a surgical  workup will be deemed eligible for the trial based on CT scan and bloodwork.
Eligible dogs will undergo the PDT therapy under anesthesia, where a drug called verteporfin is injected into the liver tumor under CT guidance.  Dogs will be monitored in the ICU for two nights until standard surgical resection of the liver tumor on the second day after PDT therapyBlood samples will be collected during the hospital stay
Compensation
Costs associated with diagnosing the liver tumor are not covered by the study.  Once  enrolled, the study covers the cost of PDT treatment, the recheck CT scans, hospital stay charge during the 2-day study period between PDT treatment and liver tumor removal surgery. The study also pays $2000 towards the cost of standard liver tumor removal surgery. The study does not cover costs associated with any complications from PDT treatment. Owners are responsible for their own travel costs.
