Patient Disease: Treatment of canine multicentric lymphoma

Study Title: Assessing the efficacy and tolerability of daunomustine in dogs with multicentric
lymphoma.

Purpose of Study/Background/Objectives: The purpose of this study is to evaluate the
efficacy of a novel chemotherapy drug, daunomustine, in treatment naïve dogs with multicentric
lymphoma.

Inclusion Criteria:

   •   Cytologic or histologic diagnosis of multicentric lymphoma from a peripheral node
   •   Treatment naïve including steroids
   •   Adequate organ function
   •   Favorable performance status

Exclusion Criteria:

   •   Dogs weighing < 4 kgs or that are < 1 yr of age
   •   Indolent or small cell lymphoma
   •   Previous chemotherapy or steroid therapy (use of steroid-containing topicals allowed)
   •   Liver values > 4x normal or elevated total bilirubin
   •   BUN and Creatinine > 1.5x normal. Minerals (excluding calcium), electrolytes, and
       albumin within 20% of the margins of normal range. Hypercalcemic dogs can enroll if
       they feel well and are otherwise eligible.
   •   Thrombocytopenia < 100,000, Neutropenia < 2,000
   •   Dilated cardiomyopathy (left ventricular dilation and systolic dysfunction) or history of
       congestive heart failure

Eligibility Diagnostics:

   •   CBC, Chemistry Panel, U/A
   •   Cytology or histopathologic diagnosis of lymphoma

Treatment/Protocol: The patient will receive up to 5 doses of IV daunomustine given every 14
days. At each treatment, dose will be escalated within the patient based on response. Patients
will return to the U of I seven days after each treatment for bloodwork and exam as deem
necessary. Following completion of the 5 doses the patient will return every 2 weeks for 2 visits,
then every 2 months for 2 visits, then every 3 months long term, where exam and lab work will
be performed. A single dose of l-asparaginase will be given as deemed appropriate during the
protocol for patients not in complete remission. Patients will be treated on an outpatient basis
and go home each day. Patients will be removed from trial and offered standard of care if they
are assessed as having progressive disease.
