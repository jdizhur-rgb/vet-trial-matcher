All dogs that meet the study criteria will receive the study drug (no placebo) followed by surgical removal of the tumor 1 week later. Study Purpose: This clinical trial evaluates the safety, feasibility, and biological effects of a newly designed immunotherapy. Specifically, the study is evaluating the injection of investigational T-cell engagers (TCEs) directly into tumors (intratumorally) in canine patients with soft tissue sarcoma (STS). TCEs are engineered antibodies that physically connect T cells to cancer cells by targeting specific markers (CD3 or CD28 on T cells, and B7-H3 or PD-L1 on cancer cells), triggering the immune system to destroy tumor cells. Similar therapies have shown positive results in cancers of blood cells (e.g. leukemia), however, they have had more limited success in solid tumors. This is in part because it is hard to get enough of the drug into the tumor when it is given through the blood stream (intravenously) without causing side effects. Delivering this dual-TCE combination directly into the tumor aims to achieve localized anti-cancer activity at lower individual doses, reducing the potential for severe whole-body side effects. Treatment Objectives The clinical trial is designed to test varying TCE doses and formulations. A fluorescent tracking dye is mixed into the injection to trace placement and to aid in assessing the anti-tumor response to the therapy. Following TCE injection, as part of the standard care plan for dogs with STS, surgery is then performed to remove the tumor at VRCCO. Researchers will analyze the removed tissue via histopathology to evaluate immune cell activation, T-cell accumulation, and tumor cell death. Safety Measures, Risks, and Potential Benefits While procedures like blood draws, ultrasound, anesthesia, and surgery carry standard medical risks, the investigational TCEs also carry the potential risks of temporary discomfort, minor bleeding, redness, and irritation at the injection site. Although this study seeks to reduce these risks through careful selection of the TCEs and the intratumoral doses to be used, these and other side effects could occur including overactive immune responses/allergic reactions, gastro-intestinal upset, and other potential unforeseen adverse reactions. To mitigate these risks, all enrolled dogs receive pre-treatment diphenhydramine (Benadryl) to reduce allergic reactions, undergo overnight hospital monitoring following injection, and receive carefully adjusted dosages across patient cohorts. While enrolled dogs may not receive direct health benefits from this trial, their participation will help advance the development of vital cancer treatments for both dogs and humans. Participation in this study will include a $1,500 credit towards surgical removal of the tumor at VRCCO, along with full coverage for any ICU stays, study-related blood work, and medications required due to study participation. FOR MORE INFORMATION Past Clinical Trials CLINICAL TRIAL FOR DOGS WITH CHRONIC INFLAMMATORY ENTEROPATHY (CIE): Enrollment Now Closed Please Note: CIE can also be referred to as Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD). Dogs must have had chronic, intermittent vomiting, diarrhea, inappetence/anorexia, and/or weight loss for at least 3 weeks prior to the Screening Visit. Dogs of any age, sex, breed, or weight are eligible. All diagnostic testing for CIE (e.g., blood work, radiographs, ultrasound, endoscopy, GI biopsies) will be paid for by the study. Special diet throughout the study is also covered. Dogs must not have any other serious concurrent illness. Owners must be willing to commit to the study visit schedule over a 130-day duration. Owners will be required to assess their dog for and record clinical signs of CIE and any unfavorable events their dog experiences during the study. Dogs that are diagnosed with CIE after the screening workup will either get a novel medication for CIE or get a placebo. After the conclusion of the study (130 days) we can transition to standard therapy for CIE. This medication has been tested in healthy animals and found to be safe. All Study related costs will be covered by the trial Sponsor. FELINE CANCER STUDY: Enrollment Now Closed Veterinary Referral Center of Central Oregon concluded a clinical trial including cats with any solid tumor type that may be amenable to treatment with chemotherapy. Patients are candidates for screening even if they have received prior chemotherapy or other cancer treatments as long as they have not received any chemotherapy or radiation therapy within 3 weeks of starting the study. The study is fully funded. Enrolled patients will receive up to 4 doses of Paccal Vet® chemotherapy and tolerability and response to treatment will be monitored. What is Paccal Vet®? Paccal Vet is a proprietary formulation of paclitaxel that is being developed to be better tolerated by veterinary patients. The conventional formulation of paclitaxel (Taxol®) is associated with acute adverse reactions in dogs, which has largely precluded the use of paclitaxel as a chemotherapeutic in veterinary oncology. Paclitaxel is one of the most effective anticancer agents used in human cancer today, with potent activity against a wide variety of tumors. These studies seek to test whether PaccalVet is safe for use in dogs and cats, and whether it has antitumor activity against certain cancers. What to Expect? Cats enrolled in the study will receive Paccal Vet via intravenous infusion over 15-30 minutes once every 3 weeks for up to four doses. Throughout those treatment cycles, cats will be monitored carefully for tumor responses and adverse events through physical examinations, blood work, and quality of life questionnaires. Enrollment benefits include all costs associated with treatment as well as diagnostics, including lab work, imaging (x-rays and ultrasound) and examinations during the study. LEARN MORE ABOUT THIS TRIAL HEMANGIOSARCOMA CLINICAL STUDY Enrollment Now Closed Veterinary Referral Center of Central Oregon has completed enrollment for dogs with Stage I or II splenic hemangiosarcoma (HSA) that have recently undergone splenectomy. Patients are candidates for screening as long as they can initiate study treatment within 30 days of splenectomy and have not received cancer therapy to date. The study is fully funded. Enrolled patients will receive 4 doses of Paccal Vet® chemotherapy and will be monitored for up to 12 months following splenectomy. This study has been reviewed and approved by an ethical review board and will be conducted in compliance with VICH-GCP guidelines. It is being run by CASTR Alliance Inc., a veterinary clinical research organization. Enrollment in this study is voluntary. What is Paccal Vet®? Paccal Vet is a proprietary formulation of paclitaxel that is being developed to be better tolerated by veterinary patients. The conventional formulation of paclitaxel (Taxol®) is associated with acute adverse reactions in dogs, which has largely precluded the use of paclitaxel as a chemotherapeutic in veterinary oncology. Paclitaxel is one of the most effective anticancer agents used in human cancer today, with potent activity against a wide variety of tumors. While anti-tumor activity has been documented in other cancers in dogs, studies have not yet been performed in hemangiosarcoma. This study seeks to test whether Paccal Vet is safe for use in dogs with hemangiosarcoma and whether the drug may improve their survival. What to Expect? Each patient will be actively participating in the study for up to 12 months from the time of splenectomy or until disease progression. Enrolled patients will receive a total of 4 doses of Paccal Vet, administered 3 weeks apart. Enrollment benefits include all costs associated with treatment as well as diagnostics, including lab work, imaging (x-rays and ultrasound) and examinations during the study.
