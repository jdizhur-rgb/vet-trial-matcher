Cancer screening Blood Test for Any Dog Over 20# Cancer test flyer Cancer test consent form We are offering a free cancer screening test for any dog over 20#. They don’t need to be previous patients at UW Veterinary Care. The CancerK9 test is a screening tool that measure DNA fragments in the blood. The results are sent to your regular veterinarian who may call or email to consult with the team to help interpret the results which take 2-3 weeks. There are 3 result types: 1. No evidence of cancer 2. ”Gray area” – may be cancer, but more often infectious or inflammatory conditions (Ex: chronic allergies, ear infections, UTI’s, or persistent Lyme antibodies), recommend retesting 3-6 months after treatment 3. Cancer likely – recommend follow up with your regular vet to get samples of any known masses, a chest x-ray, and abdominal ultrasound to look for internal signs of cancer. If interested in scheduling, please email clinicalstudies@vetmed.wisc.edu . NEW – Targeted Radiopharmaceutical Therapy (RPT) in Companion Dogs with Peripheral T-Cell Lymphoma (PTCL) In collaboration with the UW Carbone Cancer Center, we are developing an innovative treatment for canine Peripheral T-Cell Lymphoma (PTCL). This study aims to evaluate the safety, effectiveness, and optimal dosage of ⁹⁰Y-NM600 targeted radiopharmaceutical therapy (RPT) in dogs with PTCL. Our research indicates that targeted RPT can effectively reach tumors, regardless of tumor location. Please note that this is not a double-masked trial; ALL enrolled patients will receive the targeted RPT. This funded study (after initial screening) covers the following expenses: *All required exam visits EXCEPT the initial screening visit *All laboratory work (CBC, blood chemistry, urinalysis), EXCLUDING the initial lab work needed for screening. *Tumor biopsy. *PET-CT scans. *Hospitalization. *All costs related to RPT. *Additionally, up to $1,000 is available to help manage any treatment-related side effects, should they occur. *Please be aware that there is no financial incentive for enrolling your pet in this trial. Dogs showing a positive response or stable disease at the day 21 assessment may be eligible for an additional ⁹⁰Y-NM600 dose. Dogs with progressive disease will be withdrawn from the study and offered either standard-of-care chemotherapy (at the client’s expense) or treatment discontinuation, based on the client’s preference. Please note that standard-of-care chemotherapy is NOT part of the trial. Inclusion Criteria Dogs with flow cytometric or immunohistochemical (IHC)-confirmed PTCL (any stage, as long as the tumor can be sampled). Must have a measurable lesion accessible to biopsy. Patient older than one (1) year of age. ≥ 10 kg body weight. Signed owner informed consent. Dogs with VCOG-CTCAE constitutional signs ≤ 1 Exclusion Criteria Dogs without flow cytometric or IHC-confirmed PTCL. Dogs without a measurable tumor accessible for biopsy. Dogs younger than one year. Dogs weighing less than 10 kg. Pregnant or lactating dogs. Dogs with VCOG-CTCAE constitutional signs > 1. Dogs with significant comorbidities that make it unlikely they can complete the trial For additional information please call the Oncology Service at (608) 890-0422 or email at oncclinicaltrials@vetmed.wisc.edu. FLASH Radiotherapy for Canine Osteosarcoma FLASH flyer FLASH Consent Form FLASH radiation is defined as ultra-high dose rate radiation which has been shown in small animal and tissue models to spare normal tissues to a greater extent than normal radiation. Any dog with suspect osteosarcoma on a limb will qualify. 1 treatment of radiation will be performed. Then your dog will be randomized to the FLASH group or the conventional radiation group for treatments. They will have 4 once/week follow up visits. If amputation is elected, it will be performed 1 month after the start of the study and the cost is covered by the study. For more information, contact Dr. Tricia Hu at tghu@wisc.edu or Dr. Nathaniel Van Asselt at nvanasselt@wisc.edu or call 608-890-0422. Visbiome Vs Metronidazole for diarrhea in dogs with lymphoma on CHOP Metro vs. Visbiome Consent Vis vs Metro Flyer 25 The UWVC Oncology service is evaluating the efficacy of a probiotic (Visbiome Vet®), compared to traditionally used metronidazole for the management of chemotherapy induced diarrhea in dogs with multicentric lymphoma receiving a 19-week CHOP chemotherapy protocol.Dogs with a confirmed diagnosis of multicentric lymphoma whose owners present to the UWVC oncology service for consultation and elect to treat their dog with 19-week CHOP protocol will be given the option to participate in the clinical trial. Dogs will be randomized into either the Visbiome® Vet or metronidazole treatment groups. Owners of patients in both groups will be instructed to keep a diary once enrolled in the trial to monitor stool frequency, consistency, and schedule. Diet and medications will also be recorded; owners will be instructed to keep their dog’s diet consistent. Owners will start the respective diarrhea management medication once stools reach a fecal score of >4 (Fecal Scoring System, Nestle Purina PetCare Company, St. Louis, MO), or increased frequency or urgency from baseline is noted. The Fecal Scoring System will be provided for owners, and they will also be encouraged to take photos of their dogs’ eliminations. Stool samples will be collected at baseline/enrollment, prior to each doxorubicin administration, and 1 month after completion of chemotherapy. These samples will be analyzed in future research with consultation with the Gastrointestinal lab at Texas A&M University. Owners will be financially responsible all costs associated with diagnosis of multicentric lymphoma and treatment with CHOP chemotherapy with the exception of recheck examination fees for timepoints associated with fecal sample collection, which will be covered by the study as client incentive. The cost of metronidazole or Visbiome® Vet (depending upon their randomized group) will be paid for by the study. For further information or to enroll a potential patient, please contact oncclinicaltrials@vetmed.wisc.edu or call 608-890-0422.
