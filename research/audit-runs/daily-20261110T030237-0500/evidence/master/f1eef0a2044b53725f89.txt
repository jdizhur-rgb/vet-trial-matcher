Atherton Laboratory Back Our Research Homepage Research Research Laboratories Atherton Laboratory Our Research We are interested in defining the prognostic and therapeutic role of T cells in hematologic malignancies utilizing a multispecies comparative approach. Our research involves identification and appraisal of novel strategies for T cell-based immunotherapy in clinical trials of canine cancer patients with tumors bearing translational importance for human medicine. Learn more about our research Clinical Trials Evaluation of Novel CAR T Therapy for Canine Histiocytic Sarcoma In this study we will investigate the safety and therapeutic activity of a type of engineered white blood cell known as a CAR T cell. CAR T cells are made from T cells collected from your dog. In this study, we will engineer T cells to express a cancer targeting receptor known as a chimeric antigen receptor (CAR). The CAR directs the modified T cells to the cancer cells which are then killed. The purpose of this study is to determine the safety of CAR T cells, their maximum tolerated dose, how long they will remain in the patient and their effectiveness against histiocytic sarcoma. For more information , please email vet-cancertrials@vet.upenn.edu .
