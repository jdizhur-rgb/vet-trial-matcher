Current Trials and Studies Bruton’s Tyrosine Kinase Inhibitor (BI705564) for Canine B‑Cell Lymphoma The University of Missouri Veterinary Health Center is enrolling dogs in a clinical trial evaluating an investigational oral Bruton’s Tyrosine Kinase (BTK) inhibitor— BI705564 —for the treatment of newly diagnosed B‑cell lymphoma. This exploratory, multicenter field study aims to assess both efficacy and safety of BTK inhibition in canine patients. Eligibility includes: Dogs >1 year old and 10–50 kg in body weight Naïve , untreated B‑cell lymphoma confirmed by cytology or histology with immunophenotyping (flow cytometry) At least two peripheral lymph nodes ≥2 cm in longest diameter WHO stage II, III, or IV B‑cell lymphoma Adequate organ function on bloodwork and urinalysis Performance score of 0–1 (normal to mildly decreased activity) and life expectancy ≥1 month Owner consent Key exclusions: T‑cell lymphoma or involvement of skin, GI tract, or nervous system Internal‑only disease (mediastinum, liver, spleen) without peripheral lymph node enlargement Prior lymphoma treatment (chemotherapy, Tanovea, Laverdia, radiation) or long‑term corticosteroids (>2 weeks) Hypercalcemia, MDR1 deficiency, pregnancy/lactation, or concurrent trial enrollment Study overview: Dogs will receive oral BI705564 once daily for 3 months (84 days) at 5 mg/kg , with possible dose escalation to 10 mg/kg in certain cases depending on early target occupancy results. The study includes scheduled evaluations such as: Physical examinations and lymph node measurements Quality‑of‑life assessments Bloodwork (CBC, chemistry, lymphoma testing) Lymph node aspirates for target occupancy evaluation Thoracic radiographs and abdominal ultrasound at screening Follow‑up continues regularly throughout the 12‑week treatment period. Potential benefits: Access to a novel investigational BTK inhibitor designed to target B‑cell lymphoma All study‑related diagnostics, monitoring, and investigational drug costs are covered Opportunity to contribute to advancement of veterinary oncology and comparative cancer therapeutics Potential risks: Possible drug‑related side effects (as with any investigational therapy) Risks associated with repeated lymph node aspirates, blood draws, and imaging procedures Dogs may be withdrawn if adverse events occur or if continued participation is not in the animal’s best interest Owner commitment: Owners must agree to: Bring their dog for all required study visits over 12 weeks Administer oral medication daily as directed Allow required diagnostic sampling (bloodwork, lymph node aspirates, imaging) Participate in quality‑of‑life assessments Dogs may be withdrawn at any time. If a dog passes away during the study, postmortem evaluation is requested to improve understanding of treatment effects. Feline GI Lymphoma Radiation + Chemotherapy The University of Missouri Veterinary Health Center is enrolling patients into a clinical trial evaluating the safety and efficacy of whole abdomen radiation therapy (WART) followed by a chemotherapy protocol (COP/Dox) in cats with spontaneous large cell GI lymphoma. This study will seek to better understand the effects of radiation therapy prior to receiving chemotherapy for this common feline cancer. Criteria: Feline patients with high grade lymphoma in the GI tract Patients must have cytologically or histopathologically confirmed lymphoma Patients must not have been previously treated with chemotherapy Patients cannot be FeLV or FIV positive or have severe systemic disease that would place them at higher risk with general anesthesia. Patients CANNOT have evidence of lymphoma outside of the abdomen. Eligibility is at the discretion of the investigator. Benefit: The cost of the radiation portion of care (WART) will be covered by the study. Your pet will contribute to development of an important cancer therapy. Patient will receive $1,000 stipend for use at the VHC after completion of radiation therapy To Participate: Pet owners must be willing to transport their cat to and from the treatment center in Columbia, Missouri, for every visit abiding by specific timelines. A full screening including a CBC (complete blood count), serum biochemistry panel, urinalysis, thoracic radiographs (x-rays), and abdominal ultrasound will be performed prior to full study enrollment. Participation will require pets to come to our clinic weekly for a minimum of eight weeks and then every 3 weeks for 5 additional visits, with radiation administered the first week only. For more information on this study please contact: cvmclinicaltrials@missouri.edu with the subject pertaining to the trial of interest. Gilvetmab for the Treatment of Primary Pulmonary Carcinoma in Dogs The University of Missouri Veterinary Medical Center is enrolling patients into a clinical trial evaluating the effectiveness of Gilvetmab, an anti-PD1 monoclonal antibody, as a treatment for canine pulmonary carcinoma. Similar treatments have been shown to improve survival in human lung cancer patients. Criteria: Dogs with gross disease and those with microscopic disease No immune-mediated disease or other severe comorbidities No concurrent use of steroids or other immunosuppressive drugs Study Involvement: Gilvetmab is given as a slow infusion every two weeks for up to 10 doses. CBC, chemistry, and urinalysis are performed every 4 weeks. Chest x-rays are performed every 8 weeks. Benefit: The study covers the cost of Gilvetmab treatment (ranges from $540 – $1350 per treatment, depending on patient size). Owner is responsive for recheck exams, bloodwork, and x-rays. To Participate: Pet owners must be willing to transport their dog to and from the treatment center in Columbia, MO for every visit abiding by specific timelines. A full screening will be performed prior to full study enrollment. Participation will require a total of up to 10 biweekly visits. For more information on this study please contact: cvmclinicaltrials@missouri.edu with the subject pertaining to the trial of interest or fill out a short survey here. LEAH xCAR-T: Canine B-Cell Lymphoma The University of Missouri Veterinary Medical Center is enrolling patients into a clinical trial evaluating the safety and efficacy of lymphodepleting chemotherapy and xenogenic CAR-T cell therapy in dogs with spontaneous CD20+ lymphoma. CAR-T cells are lymphocytes (immune cells) that have had a series of genes added to them that make them professional killers of any cell carrying the target molecule. This study will seek to better understand the effects of the CAR-T cells on the tumor as well as the dog’s immune system. Criteria: Canine patients weighing over 10kg Patients with naive (newly diagnosed) and relapsed (pre-treated) Large Cell B-Cell lymphoma. Must be feeling clinically WELL at home. Must meet bloodwork requirements and have no evidence of primary gastrointestinal or lung involvement; eligibility is at the discretion of the investigator. Patients CANNOT be receiving immunomodulatory drugs (Apoquel, mycophenolate, cyclosporine, azathioprine). Patients must be off prednisone for a minimum of 72 hours prior to CAR-T therapy. Benefit: Complete cost of all treatments and rechecks are covered by study, including initial staging; up to $4,000 allotted for possible adverse events; $2,000 stipend for additional/future treatment at the VHC upon completion You pet will contribute to development of an important cancer therapy. To Participate: Pet owners must be willing to transport their dog to and from the treatment center in Columbia, Missouri, for every visit abiding by specific timelines. A full screening will be performed prior to full study enrollment. Participation will require pets to come to our clinic for a minimum of seven visits over the first 21 days of the study, with future longer-term follow up appointments required, and/or until progression or relapse. For more information on this study please contact: cvmclinicaltrials@missouri.edu with the subject pertaining to the trial of interest. Thyroid Sodium Iodide Symporter The University of Missouri Veterinary Medical Center is enrolling patients into a clinical trial to assess whether using special stains for the sodium iodide symporter can allow us to identify tumors that are taking up iodine after a thyroid tumor has been removed. This would limit the need for expensive thyroid scans before surgery and increase the number of dogs that could be considered for additional treatment if their thyroid cancer is aggressive. Criteria : Dogs with presumed thyroid carcinoma considered likely candidates for thyroidectomy Patients who have received neoadjuvant radiation (external beam or I-131) or chemotherapy, exogenous iodine supplementation are not eligible. Benefit: The pre-operative thyroid scan would allow radioactive iodine therapy to be considered as an option following surgery, if there were concerns that your dog’s thyroid tumor was more aggressive than usual. After enrollment, the study will contribute $1,500 to cover associated clinical costs. This includes coverage of costs associated with the pre-operative thyroid scan, including sedation and hospital care (approximately ($1,055). To Participate: Pet owners must be willing to move forward with surgical planning and resection of thyroid and surrounding lymph nodes. A full screening will be performed prior to enrollment at the owner’s expense. Responsible for initial assessment and consultation to determine eligibility for the trial. For more information on this study please contact: cvmclinicaltrials@missouri.edu with the subject pertaining to the trial of interest.
