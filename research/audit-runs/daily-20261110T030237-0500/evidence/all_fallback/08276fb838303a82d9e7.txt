Current status: Open and enrolling Study title Evaluation of xenogeneic CAR-T cell therapy for canine CD20+ B cell lymphoma Purpose of study The current standard of care for canine B-cell lymphoma is a multi-agent (CHOP) chemotherapy protocol, which require weekly trips to the vet over approximately 5 months, costs $7k-12k, and puts 80-90% of dogs into remission for 9-12 months before relapse. Canine B-cell lymphoma resembles human non-Hodgkin’s lymphoma, a cancer now successfully treated with Chimeric Antigen Receptor T cells (CAR-T cells). In this study, LEAH Labs is sponsoring an pilot trial to test the safety of a CAR-T cell therapy product in dogs with B-cell lymphoma; with the hopes to find a more cost-effective and efficacious therapy for dogs with this disease. Eligibility criteria Eligible dogs must match these criteria: body weight of at least 10kgs (22lbs) any breed, except boxers Clinical presentation: Peripheral multicentric lymphoma (minimum of 4 target peripheral lymph nodes evaluable) Have naïve or relapsed disease, substage A only fine needle aspirate (in-house or reference lab) consistent with lymphoblastic/high grade lymphoma performance status 0-1 Concurrent medications of note: Immunomodulatory drugs (Apoquel, mycophenolate, cyclosporine, azathioprine) are not allowed Cytopoint is allowable and can be maintained for the study period Prednisone use: not allowed if it's used for substage B disease patient must be able to taper off 72 hours prior to CAR-T administration and remain off for duration of study Procedures Screening visit(s): Physical exam (PE), blood and urine tests, fine needle aspirates (FNA), lymph node measurements, chest x-rays, abdominal ultrasound Pre-treatment visits (Days -5 to -2): PE, blood for CBC, fluids (before and after treatment), lymph node measurements, support medication (in hospital and sent home with), Pentostatin bolus Day -3: along with above, IV catheter placed, Cyclophosphamide and Furosemide infusion; overnight hospital stay for observation CAR-T administration (Day 0): PE, blood tests, lymph node measurements, IV catheter place, CAR-T infusion and injection in lymph nodes Follow-up visits (Day +7, Day +21): PE, blood tests, lymph node measurements; UA, chest x-rays and abdominal ultrasound on day +21 Post CAR-T visits (2 and 3 months): PE, blood tests, chest x-rays, abdominal ultrasound Unscheduled visits: if your dog needs to be seen between the study visits above, they may consist of a PE, blood and urine tests, lymph node measurements, FNA At any time the dog can be hospitalized in ICU for supportive fluids or other procedures as needed Note, urinalysis may be added to recheck visits if clinical signs indicate it is needed Costs This study is fully funded and will cover the costs of visits as outlined above, including the screening visit. A $2,000 stipend upon completion of the study or, If at any time, animal welfare in relation to disease or tumor progression necessitates early study departure. This stipend can only be used at the University of Minnesota Veterinary Medical Center for future cancer care and monitoring. Appropriate medications will be provided for your dog up to $4,000 if he/she develops any severe side effects due to the CAR-T treatment that require medical management, when care is provided at the University of Minnesota's Veterinary Medical Center.
