K9-ACV has been safely used in over 2,000 canine patients. Previous studies (without the checkpoint inhibitor) have demonstrated strong safety and encouraging outcomes. This trial reflects a growing focus on biologic therapies that harness the body’s own healing mechanisms—providing a more targeted, immune-driven approach to cancer treatment. Study Process: Following amputation, patients are randomized to receive either the K9-ACV combination therapy or carboplatin chemotherapy. The K9-ACV group receives an intradermal checkpoint inhibitor followed by vaccine administration over three doses, spaced 21 days apart. Monitoring includes exams, blood work, and imaging at 4, 6, and 8 months. Inclusion Criteria: Dogs ≥ 1 year old, ≥ 20 kg Confirmed diagnosis of Stage IIB appendicular osteosarcoma; histopathology confirmation determined through study following amputation Good general health, with no recent immunosuppressive or chemotherapeutic treatments Questions or interest in joining the study? These samples will help develop and validate new testing methods.  Ethos PUSH Study (Precision Medicine Umbrella Study for Hemangiosarcoma) Current Status: Open Study Goals: The Ethos-PUSH study seeks to deliver a highly advanced program investigating the use of novel drugs for the treatment of hemangiosarcoma (HSA) with the goal of curing this aggressive cancer. Ethos Discovery’s previous work in HSA has taught us that HSA is not a single cancer, but rather at least four different subtypes of cancer, each characterized by a different genomic mutation.  Previous studies evaluating drugs for the treatment of HSA have shown variable results, and the discovery of these different subtypes could explain this phenomenon. This study will ask if drugs specifically matched to these genomic subtypes of patients with HSA can improve their outcomes.  Click here for more details
