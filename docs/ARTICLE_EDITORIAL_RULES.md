# Vet Trial Finder article rules

## Voice and structure

- Write for an intelligent pet owner, not for a specialist and not for a child.
- Use plain English and explain a technical term the first time it appears.
- Prefer short sentences and ordinary punctuation. Avoid long em dashes.
- Open with a direct explanation of the treatment or subject and why an owner may be considering it.
- Use descriptive headings, short paragraphs and lists only where they make scanning easier.
- Distinguish clearly between licensed treatment, treatment used in clinical practice, active clinical trials and preclinical research.
- State important limitations, uncertainty and practical questions for the treating team.
- Do not repeat catalog information already shown in a separate trial or center block.
- End with a concise summary and a list of primary or authoritative sources.

## Evidence and claims

- Prefer peer-reviewed research, official clinical guidance, regulators, universities, veterinary hospitals and study teams.
- Do not turn an early study, laboratory result or company announcement into a claim of clinical benefit.
- Name the species, cancer type and clinical setting to which a result applies.
- Verify claims about approval, availability, enrollment and commercial status before publication.
- Link readers to the relevant Vet Trial Finder search or directory when the site has a useful matching tool.
